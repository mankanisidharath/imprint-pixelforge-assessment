PixelForge assessment guide

Generate images with Pixelforge, review an estimated charge before submitting, and follow the result in History. Usage shows a daily breakdown of your finished generations and charges.

Creating an image

  1. Choose Generate and enter a prompt of up to 4,000 characters.
  2. Select a size and quality, and optionally add up to four reference image URLs.
  3. Review the estimate and choose Generate.

Try these prompts

Enter these on the Generate page, then check the outcome in History.

  • [succeed] a red fox — succeeds and is charged.
  • [fail] a castle — fails during processing and is not charged.
  • [succeed] [duplicate] a boat — sends duplicate webhook events; the generation is charged once.
  • [slow] [succeed] — submit five times in quick succession to exercise concurrency waiting.
  • [blocked] test and [overloaded] test — show failure messages for a blocked prompt and an unavailable provider.

How to read costs

The estimate is a range that includes a 25% service fee; when available, the Generate button shows its maximum. The final charge depends on the tokens used and appears in History when the image finishes. Hover over the displayed charge to see the exact nine-decimal value. Failed generations are not charged.

History

History shows your prompts, images, sizes, quality, charges, and creation times. Pending generations update automatically while the page is open.

  • Queued: Waiting to start; a note may explain the wait.
  • Processing: The image is being generated.
  • Succeeded: The image and final charge are ready. Select the image to open it at full size.
  • Failed: The generation was not charged. Review the error and select Try again to return to the form.

Image links last 24 hours. Use the pagination controls to browse older results.

Usage

Usage shows your finished generations and exact charges for the last 30 UTC days, with a daily breakdown. Local submission failures without a completion date are counted on their last update date. Pending generations remain in History.

Account settings

Open the sidebar user menu to manage your profile and account settings or sign out.

Architecture in one screen

  • Nanodollars keep stored amounts precise.
  • One settlement function handles provider updates from submissions, webhooks, and reconciliation.
  • Event deduplication records each webhook event at most once.
  • A row lock protects settlement from competing updates.
  • Polling fallback checks unfinished generations when webhook updates are missed.

Data

The documented production setup uses ClickHouse Managed Postgres for the application and ClickHouse for analytics. See the analytics documentation for the queries.
